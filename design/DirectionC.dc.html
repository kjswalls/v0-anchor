What's the tone of the Q2 one?
Direct, three sections, no preamble.
Ask OpenClaw…
No panel floats over the page any more. Every chip here is live, so nothing is rendered twice — the read-only StaticChip component disappears, and so does the locally-mounted second ItemDialog.
Why
Because a docked inspector 420px wide, sitting beside a live canvas, is the wrong place to configure a recurrence rule or join a program — those are decisions about the shape of your weeks, and you make them looking at the item, not at Tuesday. Splitting by frequency of use rather than by screen size is the only split that gives the two surfaces different jobs, which is what would justify keeping both. It also retires the flat chip row entirely: the panel gets three chips and a sentence, the page gets room to group properly.
Tradeoff — the sharpest of the five
You have to be right about which three. Get it wrong and every session is a route change: click the row, read, click through to the page, lose the canvas, come back. Anchor's whole panel design was built the other way — non-modal, compressing the day rather than covering it, precisely so you never leave the grid — and this direction spends that on any edit outside the three. It is also the most work: a genuine second layout, not a re-grouping, plus a real answer for mobile, where "go to the page" is the only option and the inspector has no canvas to sit beside.
Sidebar and full page: two, and for the first time genuinely different
This is the only direction where the answer is emphatically two and the two are not the same thing at different widths. Today they are one component with a costume; here they become an inspector and an editor — different content, different jobs, no shared property row to fall out of sync. The cost of that clarity is that the page stops being optional: it becomes load-bearing, and a route change becomes part of ordinary editing.
