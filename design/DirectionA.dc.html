Goal
Ordered by what this account actually uses, not by source order. Everything the registry allows is in the list, so nothing is deleted — it is one keystroke away instead of always on screen.
Why
Because the measured weight is not the chips, it is the always-on empty scaffolding: Subtasks, Agent and Thread each render a heading and a control for every item, used or not. A dashed unset chip and an empty section heading are both the app saying "you could" to someone who did not ask. This is the smallest change that removes the most pixels, and it needs no new concepts — the chip vocabulary, the popover and the registry gates all stay exactly as they are.
Tradeoff — and it is a real one
The dashed unset chip is how you discover Anchor's model. Hide Repeat behind a + and a user who has never made a recurring task has no longer any reason to think one exists. That is the exact opposite of what the extension gating is for: gating removed concepts you have not bought; this removes concepts you already own. It also trades a one-click edit for a two-click one on every property you have not set yet, forever — worst on mobile, where the + menu is another sheet on top of a sheet.
Sidebar and full page: two, unchanged
This direction does not touch the split. The panel earns its rows; the page shows every band expanded by default, because a page you navigated to is a place you meant to go — the disclosure rule inverts there rather than repeating. The doubling on /item/[id] survives untouched, which is this direction's honest limit: it makes the panel lighter and leaves the ticket's actual question unanswered.
