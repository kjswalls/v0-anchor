A fresh braindump item is one line, and it still says everything true about itself. Nine dashed chips said the same thing in five rows.
Why
Because it is the shape most like the rest of the app, and because it fixes the one thing a grouped row cannot: a set of chips has no grammar. "Onboarding · Deep work · Ship v2" are three identical pills for three completely different relationships — filed under, gated by, in service of. A sentence has to name the relationship to be a sentence at all, so the container-registry's three roles get expressed for free, in words the user already uses. It also collapses to almost nothing when the item is almost nothing, without hiding anything behind a +.
Tradeoff
Sentences do not scan for absence. A chip row lets your eye find the empty slot; a paragraph makes you read to the end to notice there is no date — the focused ghost clauses are a patch on that, not a fix. Prose is also brittle where a row is not: it reflows badly at 294px, it needs a real answer for a value that is a 40-character project name, and every clause needs an accessible name and role that a bare <button> chip got for free. And it is the least reversible of the five — half a sentence is nothing, so this cannot ship a section at a time.
Sidebar and full page: one, because the difference stops mattering
The sentence is the summary, so there is nothing left for a read-only rendering to do — the page's StaticChips go, and with them the doubling. What remains is a route that shows the same sentence with the thread and the subtasks laid out beside it rather than under it, and a panel that stacks them. Different layout, identical content, one component. The one thing the page keeps that the panel cannot is the URL, which is the honest reason to keep the route at all.
